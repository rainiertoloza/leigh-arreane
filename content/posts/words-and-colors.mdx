People sometimes ask if I am a writer who paints, or a painter who writes. I never know how to choose. At a young age I fell in love with the art of storytelling through words and colors, and I have not been able to put either of them down.

A sentence can hold a whole country. A canvas can hold a silence you cannot spell. When I am stuck in a chapter, I mix a color. When a painting will not tell me what it wants, I write until the image arrives. They argue with each other, and then they agree.

**Dreams We Once Lost** is a novel, but it was also a visual problem: How do you show a life split between Sweden and the Philippines without turning either place into a postcard? How do you paint a stranger who looks like a lost friend? I do not think I solved it perfectly. I do not think I was supposed to. The book is perfectly imperfect, the way a canvas is when you stop just before you overwork it.

I still dream of sharing this passion with as many people as I can — not as a finished brand, but as an invitation. If you write, if you paint, if you do both in secret: sit them at the same table. Let them talk.
