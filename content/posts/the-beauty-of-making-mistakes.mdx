I learned painting the way I learned writing: by doing it wrong first.

There was no teacher standing over my shoulder, no syllabus that said this is how a canvas should look. Only the quiet of a room, a color I could not name yet, and the stubborn wish to make something that felt true. Self-taught is a lonely phrase until you realize it also means free. Free to smudge. Free to start over. Free to keep a piece that nobody else would call finished.

I create random oeuvres — a paragraph one morning, a wash of color the next. I used to be embarrassed by that restlessness. Now I think it is the same instinct. Words and colors are two ways of asking the same question: what is this feeling, and where does it belong?

When I wrote **Dreams We Once Lost**, I wanted the story to hold that same permission. Laura is not a heroine who never stumbles. She blames herself. She loses things. She looks at the moon and wonders about the sun. The book is, in many ways, a love letter to the courage of trying again — not because the first attempt failed, but because living asks more than one draft.

If you are waiting to be good enough before you begin, I hope you will begin anyway. The mistakes are not the opposite of the art. They are how the art learns your hand.
