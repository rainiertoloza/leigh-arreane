There is a line I return to whenever someone asks what the book is about.

Will you find your sun if you’re looking at the moon?

It is not a riddle I wrote to be clever. It is the feeling of standing in the wrong light and still hoping you will recognize yourself. Laura Wills dreams like the rest of us — until the dreams become a prelude to what actually happens, and then they leave. A father. An almost-love. A country that is home and also not. A stranger named Sam who should have stayed in memory, and does not.

I am a Filipino author. I know what it is to write from more than one map at once. I will not pretend Laura’s life is mine, beat for beat. Fiction is allowed to borrow the weather of a life without stealing the house. What I did take honestly is the work of finding a space you can call your own — not a passport stamp, not a perfect plan, but a room inside yourself that does not vanish when the light changes.

If you have ever blamed yourself for the mishaps, if you have ever gone back to an old hometown looking for a missing piece, I wrote this for that version of you. The one who is still looking. The one who might, one day, look up and find that the sun was never as far as it seemed.
